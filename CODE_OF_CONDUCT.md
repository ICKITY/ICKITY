# ICKITY — Contributor & Editorial Code of Conduct

ICKITY is an open source encyclopedia built by a distributed team of
writers, editors, designers, and engineers. This document sets the standards
for every contribution, whether it is a new article, a correction to an
existing one, a design change, or a code fix. By contributing to this
repository you agree to abide by this Code of Conduct.

## 1. Our Editorial Mission

ICKITY publishes long-form, Wikipedia-style encyclopedia articles
that are accurate, neutral, and richly interlinked. Every article must be
written in full — never as a placeholder, stub, or abbreviated summary — and
must meet the minimum length and structure standards described in the
repository README.

## 2. Factual Accuracy

- Every factual claim in an article must be verifiable against reliable,
  published sources. Contributors must cite their sources in the numbered
  References section of each article.
- Contributors must not invent dates, names, quotations, statistics, or
  citations. If a fact cannot be verified, it must be omitted or clearly
  qualified.
- When a factual error is reported, the contributor who introduced it — or any
  other editor — must correct it promptly and record the correction in the
  pull request description.
- Articles about living people, contested history, and scientific consensus
  must present the mainstream scholarly view first, with minority views
  attributed and clearly labelled.

## 3. Authentic Real-World Media Verification

- Every image, photograph, and video used on the website or in the README
  must be a real, verified public-domain or freely licensed asset — for
  example from Wikimedia Commons, NASA archives, the Library of Congress, or
  official documentary channels.
- AI-generated images and videos are strictly prohibited anywhere in this
  repository, including articles, the README, and social media graphics.
- Before adding media, contributors must verify the source page, confirm the
  license, and record the credit line in the image caption.
- Hand-coded SVG graphics and icons are encouraged and must be original work
  created for ICKITY. No emojis may be used in the codebase,
  articles, UI, or documentation; use hand-coded inline SVGs instead.

- All code and documentation in this repository is released under the MIT

  InkCraft Social).
- Contributors retain the copyright in their contributions but grant the
  project a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free license to use, modify, and
  distribute them under the MIT License.
- Third-party media must carry a license compatible with open publication.
  Public-domain material must be identified as such in the caption and
  credits.
- Contributors must never submit material they do not have the right to
  publish, including copyrighted text copied from other encyclopedias or
  websites. Articles must be original writing informed by sources, never
  verbatim copies.

## 5. Respectful Scholarly Collaboration

- Be courteous, patient, and constructive in all project spaces: pull
  requests, issues, discussions, and social channels.
- Critique ideas and evidence, never people. Disagree with scholarship using
  scholarship — cite sources, explain reasoning, and remain open to being
  persuaded.
- Harassment, hate speech, personal attacks, trolling, and sustained
  disruption are prohibited in any form.
- New contributors are welcome. Mentors should answer basic questions
  kindly, even when they have been asked before, and should help newcomers
  find a first task.

## 6. Editorial Standards

- Articles must follow the nested folder structure
  `content/<category>/<subcategory>/<article>.json` and use the JSON schema
  documented in the README.
- Internal wiki-links must be dense and bidirectional: every article should
  link to related topics in the library, and be linked back from them.
- The browser title of every page must remain exactly `ICKITY`.
- Front-end protection features (anti-copy, anti-zoom, anti-inspect locks)
  must not be removed or weakened without a documented security review.

## 7. Enforcement

Reports of violations may be sent to the maintainers through the repository
issue tracker. Maintainers will investigate every report, protect the
reporter's privacy, and apply proportionate remedies, which may include a
warning, removal of the contribution, temporary suspension of contribution
privileges, or a permanent ban from the project.
